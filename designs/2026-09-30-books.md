# Books: every dollar in and out (2026-09-30)

William's ask: "keeps track of all business expenses and revenue i.e. does all the accounting work / EBITDA / statistic tracking for me … I'm already losing track of all the money I've spent … needs to be scalable, cover everything, and really help me organize my financials."

Status: plan. Nothing built. Stopgap: every invoice and receipt since Aug 1 is archived raw in the backups bucket under `books/` (kept, never expired), with a ledger file beside it. Step 1 imports it.

## What it answers

- Where did my money go this month, and what is waste?
- What subscriptions do I pay for, what do they cost a month, when do they renew?
- What did I earn, who owes me, what is my profit and EBITDA?
- What does each client cost me, and what does each channel cost per client won?
- How much tax do I owe, and how much should I set aside now?
- How far am I from $100k a month profit?

## Facts that shape it

- Wren is a sole proprietorship in Alberta, registered for GST. Books are in CAD, calendar year. Income goes on the T2125; GST on the GST return. You file yourself.
- Books start Aug 1 2026, when Wren's spending began.
- CRA wants business income on the accrual basis (earned when invoiced, spent when incurred). The books keep a cash view beside it.
- Spend goes on your personal BMO Mastercard. Business and personal share one card. BMO is the bank.
- Many vendors bill in USD. The card turns USD into CAD with a markup.
- Revenue terms already live in code: `@wren/offers` prices (fixed, performance) and reactivation's `amountOwed` (setup + each meeting, capped). There is no invoicing and no payment collection yet.
- Money traces today: autobrowse `charges.jsonl` (agent charges only, a handful of rows), wallet receipt mails, LLM token counts (`email_stage_costs`, tokens, no dollars), vendor emails split across your personal Gmail and william@. credvault holds cards and logins, not invoices. Nothing in wren tracks money.
- The repo is public. No amounts, card digits, GST number, addresses or client names in git. They live in the database.
- Postgres backups keep 30 days. CRA wants records kept 6 years.

## Shape

One foundation package, `@wren/books`, and a `books` schema in the main database. It knows accounts, entries, vendors, customers and documents. It never knows clients or products.

- **Products** report what a client owes through a `Billable` interface the foundation defines (reactivation: setup fee + meetings booked, capped).
- **Apps** wire it: the worker runs the loops, the CLI is `wren books`.
- **Clients** stay data: billing terms (currency, billing email, days to pay) go in the client's settings block.
- **Books as a product later**: the same schema in a client's own database. Nothing in the design is Wren-only.

Money flows through five steps, like leads through the pipeline.

1. **Capture.** Pull every raw record and keep it forever, even the ones that turn out personal.
2. **Match.** Pair each card or bank line with its receipt or invoice.
3. **Sort.** Give each line a category, a business share and a tax treatment.
4. **Post.** Write a double-entry journal entry that cites its sources.
5. **Report.** Views over the journal: P&L, cash, EBITDA, per client, per channel, tax.

### 1. Capture

| Source | What it gives | How |
|---|---|---|
| Card and bank statements | Every dollar out and in. The truth for cash. | Download as OFX/QFX/CSV, `wren books import <file>`. One parser per bank format in a registry, like CRM formats. |
| Receipts mailbox | Vendor, date, total, tax, currency, invoice number, within minutes of the charge | New `receipts@wrenautomation.com` (a free Workspace alias). Every vendor bills to it; autobrowse `RECEIPTS_TO` points at it. Read by the delegated service account like the fleet inboxes. |
| Receipts already sent to personal Gmail | History | One search by known vendor senders through your existing Gmail read consent, forwarded once. No wholesale scan of the personal inbox. |
| autobrowse charges | Agent purchases with receipt screenshots | Import `charges.jsonl` rows and PNGs as documents. |
| Vendor billing APIs | Daily usage cost before the bill lands | AWS Cost Explorer (one call a day), Anthropic cost report. Others only when a vendor's bill is big enough to watch. |
| Our own meters | Cost per run, per client | Token counts × a price table, SMS counts × rate. Closes the old "dollars need a price table" item. |
| Account inventory | Every service with a login | `autobrowse creds list`. A login with no known cost, or a cost with no login, is flagged. |

Every import is a `runs` row, checkpointed per file. A line carries a fingerprint, so importing the same statement twice changes nothing.

Documents (PDFs, emails, screenshots) go to a private S3 bucket with versioning and 7-year retention. Postgres holds the text and extracted fields.

### 2. Match

A card line matches a receipt on amount, date window and vendor. USD receipts match CAD card lines within the FX markup (about 3%). Results:

- **Matched**: done.
- **Card line with no receipt**: on the missing-receipts list; nagged after 7 days.
- **Receipt with no card line**: not on a statement yet, or paid on another card.

### 3. Sort

- **Rules first.** A rule maps a vendor pattern to a category, a business share (100%, 0%, or a split like 50% for the phone), and a tax treatment.
- **Model second.** Unknown lines get a suggested category with a reason, through `@wren/llm`.
- **You confirm.** `wren books review` shows the queue. Confirming offers "always do this", which writes a rule. The queue shrinks every month.
- **Personal lines** are kept and marked personal. They never reach the P&L.

### 4. Post

- Double-entry: every entry's lines sum to zero in CAD. A database constraint enforces it.
- Money is whole cents plus a currency code. Never floats.
- Each line keeps the original amount and currency, the CAD amount, the rate and where the rate came from (the card's own conversion, or the Bank of Canada daily rate, free from its API).
- Entries are never edited. A fix is a reversing entry plus a new one.
- A month closes once matched and reviewed, then locks.
- Every entry cites the movement, document and rule behind it. That is the audit trail CRA asks for.

Tables (in the `books` schema, so `books.accounts` never collides with autobrowse accounts or `clients.accounts`):

`entities` (Wren now; a corporation later), `accounts` (the chart, each mapped to its T2125 line and GIFI code), `vendors`, `customers`, `subscriptions`, `movements` (raw card/bank lines), `documents`, `matches`, `rules`, `entries` + `lines`, `rates`, `invoices` + `invoice_lines` + `payments`, `tax_codes`, `budgets`, `periods`.

### 5. Report

Views over `lines`, so any question is one query.

- **P&L** by month, quarter and year; by category and vendor.
- **EBITDA**: revenue minus operating costs, before interest, tax and write-offs of equipment. Shown with and without a market salary for you, since a sole proprietor's pay is not an expense.
- **Cash**: balance, monthly burn, runway.
- **Revenue**: MRR, ARR, billed vs collected, by client, offer and niche.
- **Per client**: revenue minus direct costs (its mailboxes, domains, SMS, model calls) and its share of shared infrastructure.
- **Per channel**: spend on outbound, ads and content divided by clients won there (first touch from channel attribution). Cost to win a client and months to pay it back.
- **Spend**: subscription stack total, fixed vs usage-based, top vendors, month-over-month change, FX markup paid.
- **Goal**: profit this month vs $100k, and the trend.
- **Budget vs actual** per category.

## Subscriptions

The pain today, so it gets its own table.

- **Found**: same vendor, regular gap (monthly or yearly), similar amount. Receipts that say "renewal" or "subscription" confirm it.
- **Holds**: vendor, plan, amount, currency, cycle, next renewal, card, the autobrowse login, what it is for, first seen, last used.
- **Last used**: from autobrowse sessions and wren API calls per vendor.
- **Flags**: new subscription, price change, yearly renewal in 14 days, trial ending, no use in 30 days, two tools doing one job.
- **Cancel**: an autobrowse flow per vendor, run on your yes.

## Revenue

- **Invoices** come from `Billable` each month per client: reactivation's setup fee once, then meetings booked, capped. Build offers: the upfront milestone, then the monthly retainer.
- **Sent** as a PDF from william@ through the email channel.
- **Paid** by bank transfer first. A Canadian Stripe account cannot take US bank debits (ACH), only cards, and 2.9%+ on a $15k invoice is $400+. So the invoice shows USD bank-transfer details, with a Stripe card link as the fallback. Stripe fees are posted automatically from its webhook.
- **Owed**: who owes what and for how long. Reminders at 3, 10 and 30 days late.
- **GST on invoices** by where the client is: Alberta 5%, HST provinces their rate, US clients zero-rated.

## Tax

- **GST return**: GST collected, GST paid that can be claimed back (input tax credits), net owing, per filing period. Checks that receipts over the limit show the vendor's GST number.
- **GST leak check**: some foreign vendors charge GST as if we were a consumer. That GST cannot be claimed back. The fix is giving them our GST number; the report lists who.
- **Income tax**: each category mapped to its T2125 line. Equipment (the Mac) on a capital cost schedule, written off over years. Home office and phone at their business share.
- **Set aside**: estimated income tax and CPP (both halves when self-employed) on profit so far. The monthly amount to move into a tax account. Instalment dates once CRA requires them.
- **Year-end package** for self-filing: the number for each T2125 line and each GST return line, ready to type into tax software. Beside it: equipment schedule, personal-share adjustments, every receipt zipped. The same package serves an accountant later.
- **Incorporation watch**: shows when profit is high enough that a corporation would pay less tax. The decision is yours.

Tax rules here are my reading of CRA guidance. Check them against CRA's guides before the first return, or pay an accountant for one review.

## Alerts

Through the existing notifier (text and email):

- A new vendor or new subscription
- A charge over twice that vendor's usual
- The same charge twice
- A yearly renewal or trial end within 14 days
- A card line with no receipt after 7 days
- An invoice overdue
- A category over its budget (AWS, ads)

## Surfaces

- **CLI**: `wren books import | review | spend | subs | owed | pnl | tax | close | export`.
- **Weekly report**: a money section. Spent this week, new charges, profit so far this month, owed to you, renewals in the next 14 days, missing receipts.
- **Dashboard**: a books page behind Cloudflare Access, you only.
- **Ask Claude**: the `wren` skill answers "what did I spend on Google last quarter" from the views.

## Loops

- `Books/<entity>` daily: read the receipts mailbox, vendor APIs and rates; match; sort; alert.
- Monthly: invoices from billables, close reminder, subscription review.
- Yearly: archive the closed year (journal export + documents) to the 7-year bucket.

Stays inside free tiers: one run a day, secrets read once per version.

## Where to attack

1. **One card for business and personal.** Rules handle repeat vendors; one-off buys still need your review every month. A separate card fixes it.
2. **Tax rules are unconfirmed.** GST on foreign vendors, zero-rating for US clients, equipment classes. An accountant checks before the first return.
3. **Model extraction can misread receipts.** Matching to the card line catches a wrong total, not a wrong category. Review catches that.
4. **Monthly statements lag.** Until the statement lands, a vendor that sends no receipt is invisible. Daily alerts rest on receipts.
5. **FX tolerance.** Card CAD includes the bank's markup; a 3% window can pair two similar charges wrongly. Vendor and date must agree too.
6. **Cost per client is a model.** Shared infrastructure split by usage share. A bad split shows a wrong margin.
7. **Retention.** Postgres backups keep 30 days. The yearly archive must exist before the first close, or old years live only in the live database. The `books/` stopgap prefix has no versioning yet.
8. **Two inboxes get bills.** Registrar, model plan and GitHub bill your personal Gmail; Workspace, Instantly, RackNerd and Telnyx bill william@. Until each vendor's billing email moves to receipts@, capture reads both.
9. **GST leak is real.** Google and Anthropic charge GST under the simplified regime, which cannot be claimed back. Giving each our GST number stops it.
